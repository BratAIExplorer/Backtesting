# Holding vs Timing: Evidence and Implications for Concentrated Small/Mid-Cap Indian Portfolios

Research date: 2026-10-19. Method: web search snippets plus 2 page fetches. Primary papers/factsheets were NOT read in full (Bessembinder PDF fetch returned binary; NSE factsheets not fetched). Everything below is secondary-source unless stated. "UNVERIFIED" = not confirmed against the primary source.

## 1. Missing the best days: how much return falls

### Takeaway
Missing just 10 best days cuts long-run CAGR by roughly a third to a half in every dataset found (S&P 500 20y: 10.4% to 6.1%; Nifty 50 21y: 13.67% to 9.75%; Nifty Midcap 150 21y: 17.23% to 13.65%). Caveat: the best days cluster next to the worst days, so a timer who exits in a crash also avoids many worst days; these studies show only the cost of missing the best, not the net of timing.

### Cited Findings
- S&P 500, 3 Jan 2005 to 31 Dec 2024 (JP Morgan Asset Mgmt data, via secondary coverage): $10,000 fully invested became about $71,750 (10.4% annualised). Missing 10 best days: about $32,871 (6.1%). — [Search summary of JPM Guide data](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/missing-just-a-few-of-the-best-stock-market-days-could-cost-you-big-2020-12-31) (older edition of same chart; 2024 figures came via search-engine summary of JPM/CNBC coverage, UNVERIFIED against JPM PDF)
- Same JPM dataset: missing 20 best days about 3.6% annualised (Greenline source); missing 60 best days leaves about $4,712 (CNBC version puts return at -3.7% for 2005-2025; period differs, exact 60-day return UNVERIFIED) — [Greenline](https://usegreenline.com/articles/best-days-hide-inside-the-worst); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/03/09/you-may-miss-the-markets-best-days-if-you-sell-amid-high-volatility.html)
- JPM: 7 of the 10 best days occurred within two weeks of the 10 worst days (past 20 years). — same JPM coverage as above
- Older JPM editions: 1999-2018, missing the 10 best days halved the return; 2000-2019, annualised return fell from 6% to 2.4%. — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2019/04/11/what-happens-when-you-miss-the-best-days-in-the-st.aspx) (UNVERIFIED, search summary)
- Nifty 50, Apr 2005 to Jul 2026 (Abakkus MF study): all days 13.67% CAGR; miss 10 best 9.75%; miss 30 best 4.68%. — [Business Today / Abakkus](https://www.businesstoday.in/mutual-funds/story/nifty-midcap-150-falls-over-20-once-every-4-2-years-abakkus-study-555987-2026-09-17) (search summary for Nifty 50 figures; fund-house source, may be promotional)
- Nifty 50 TRI 2005-2025 (FundsIndia via Business Standard): Rs 10 lakh to about Rs 1.43 crore; missing 10 best days about Rs 67 lakh. Seven of the 10 best days within two weeks of the 10 worst. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/invested-right-before-market-crash-history-says-you-will-still-make-money-125041400079_1.html)
- Nifty in USD (CAIA India), roughly 23-24 years: missing 10 best days cut USD return from about 8.4% to 4.7%. — [CAIA](https://caia.org/node/7206) (exact period UNVERIFIED)
- Nifty Midcap 150 TRI, Apr 2005 to 31 Aug 2026 (Abakkus, fetched and read): Rs 10,000 became Rs 3,00,810 (17.23% CAGR). Miss best 5 days: Rs 2,05,241 (15.15%), about 31% less wealth. Miss 10: Rs 1,54,804 (13.65%). Miss 30: Rs 66,638 (9.26%). Miss 50: Rs 33,258 (5.77%). — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/mutual-funds/story/nifty-midcap-150-falls-over-20-once-every-4-2-years-abakkus-study-555987-2026-09-17)
- Upstox: over 15 years, missing the best 10 days gave a 53% lower return than buy-and-hold (period/index not confirmed). — [Upstox](https://community.upstox.com/t/upstox-point-of-view-don-t-try-to-time-the-market-stay-the-course/99) (UNVERIFIED)

### Inferences
- The "miss best N days" test is one-sided. It does not show the benefit of also dodging the worst days; JPM's own point is that the two are adjacent, so a rule that exits on weakness often misses both. A fair test is a rule backtest (section 3), not this chart.
- 5 best days cost about 2 CAGR points on Midcap 150 (17.23% to 15.15%). A rule that is out of the market during sharp rebounds (e.g. Mar-Apr 2020) pays this cost.

### Gaps
- Nifty 50 / Sensex figures for 5 and 20 best days were not found with a stated period (only 10 and 30). Nifty Smallcap 250 miss-best-days not found.
- No primary JPM Guide to the Markets or niftyindices.com data was opened.
- All Indian studies come from fund houses or brokers (sales motive). No independent academic Nifty "best days" study found.

## 2. Concentration of returns in few winners (Bessembinder)

### Takeaway
Bessembinder (2018, JFE) found that US stock wealth creation above T-bills came from about 4% of stocks; most stocks lose to T-bills over their lifetime. Selling a winner early risks removing the very stock that makes the portfolio's return. The same result also means a concentrated portfolio most likely holds no such winner.

### Cited Findings
- US common stocks, 1926-2016: best-performing about 4% of listed companies (1,092 of nearly 26,000, 4.3%) explain the entire net gain of the US market over one-month T-bills; the other 96% collectively matched T-bills. Net wealth created above T-bills: about $35 trillion. — [ASU W. P. Carey summary](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20180917-do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills)
- 57.4% of stocks had lifetime buy-and-hold returns (with dividends) below one-month T-bills. — same summary via search result
- Cause: positive skewness; a few very large returns lift the mean, most stocks post modest or negative lifetime returns. — same
- Follow-up, 42 countries, 1990-2018: best 811 firms (1.33% of total) accounted for all net global wealth creation. — [ASU / secondary summary](https://blogs.wpcarey.asu.edu/20180917-do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills) (the 2019 follow-up is only mentioned in search summary; UNVERIFIED, original not read)

### Inferences
- Implication for early selling: if returns come from rare multi-baggers, cutting winners at fixed targets (e.g. +30%) while letting losers run caps the right tail. This is an inference from skewness; Bessembinder does not test exit rules.
- Implication for concentrated portfolios: with 10-15 stocks and a roughly 4% base rate of "wealth-creating" stocks, a concentrated pick set probably misses the winners. This is an inference; no India-specific skew study was found.
- Bessembinder measures lifetime buy-and-hold including delisted stocks (survivorship-free in US data). It says nothing about whether holding a bad stock forever is fine; 57.4% underperformed T-bills.

### Gaps
- Primary paper PDF not parsed. Median lifetime return, top-company list (Exxon, Apple, etc.), and per-decade figures not verified.
- No Bessembinder-style study for Indian stocks found.

## 3. Trend-following / Golden Cross / moving-average timing

### Takeaway
Faber-style 10-month MA timing mainly cuts drawdowns, with return gains mixed and period-dependent. Golden Cross (50/200) evidence is weak and anecdotal; India evidence is small-sample or negative for other technical rules. Timing rules showed no proven edge here that beats buy-and-hold on return.

### Cited Findings
- Faber (2007, Journal of Wealth Management; white paper May 2006, updates 2009 and 2013): 10-month (about 200-day) SMA on asset classes, month-end signal, move to cash below it. Reported equity-like returns with bond-like volatility/drawdowns. — [Faber paper copy](https://trevcan.duckdns.org/files/trevor/papers/emintham-Papers/Faber-%20A%20Quantitative%20Approach%20to%20Tactical%20Asset%20Allocation.pdf) (copy on personal server; check vs journal)
- Faber 2018 revisit (US stocks): Sharpe 0.45 to 0.80; timing beat stocks in 2008-09, but underperformed stocks in six of the following eight years. — [Search summary of Faber update](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-why-financial-trends-persist) (UNVERIFIED)
- Faber multi-asset test: return 4.9% vs 3.5%, volatility 6.6% vs 12.8%, Sharpe 0.59 vs 0.19 (period not confirmed in snippet). — same search result group (UNVERIFIED)
- Commentary quoting Faber: drawdown fell from 46% to under 10% with timing (figure not confirmed in paper tables). — [paper copy above](https://trevcan.duckdns.org/files/trevor/papers/emintham-Papers/Faber-%20A%20Quantitative%20Approach%20to%20Tactical%20Asset%20Allocation.pdf) (UNVERIFIED)
- Faber's rule trades on month-end; choice of rebalance day can materially change results (ThinkNewfound). — [ThinkNewfound](https://blog.thinknewfound.com/2013/08/)
- South Africa, JSE indices 1961-2010: Pretoria MBA dissertation found better risk-adjusted returns even after high transaction costs. — [Univ. Pretoria](https://repository.up.ac.za/items/7f27d00d-4c0e-4826-acf0-1a7db806d25d) (student dissertation; weak authority)
- Golden Cross, Dow: SentimenTrader calls it "mostly useless" alone; better when 200-day was falling. — [SentimenTrader](https://sentimentrader.com/blog/golden-cross-with-a-rising-200-day-average)
- Golden Cross on individual stocks: 47.22% hit rate across about 40 trades, results driven by 3 trades, losses in sideways markets. — [Investagrams](https://investagrams.com/daily/2023/01/backtesting-the-golden-cross-signal) (small sample)
- India, Nifty: Emkay study (likely 2011, date unclear) counted 4 Golden Crosses after 2003, each followed by 30-78% gains (average about 47%) until the next death cross. n=4. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/technical-indicator-suggests-major-upside-for-nifty-112031600016_1.html) (UNVERIFIED, tiny sample, possible selection)
- India, Nifty Smallcap 250: July 2025 article says Golden Cross was the fifth since Jan 2019; earlier ones were followed by gains incl. a 110% rally after Aug 2020. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/golden-cross-alert-this-tech-signal-set-off-rally-on-nifty-smallcap-in-past-can-it-do-again-125070400224_1.html) (page fetch failed 403; from search summary only; "all followed by gains" is a media claim, UNVERIFIED, drawdowns during those periods not reported)
- India, academic: Muruganandan (Colombo Business Journal 2020), BSE Sensex Feb 2000-May 2018: RSI rule gave no positive return even before costs; MACD sell signals did better in bear phases pre-cost but not on a Sharpe basis; concluded abnormal returns from technical analysis are doubtful. — [CBJ](https://cbj.sljol.info/articles/56)
- Methodology warning: no comprehensive moving-average study free of data-snooping bias (Zakamulin, 155-year data). — [SSRN 2677212](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2677212)

### Inferences
- Faber's benefit is drawdown reduction, not return; the 2018 revisit's "underperformed six of eight years" is the cost (whipsaw, missing rebounds).
- A 50/200 cross is a slow, late signal: it enters after a big part of a move and exits after a big part of a decline. In sideways markets it whipsaws (Investagrams, SentimenTrader).
- India 4-5 signal samples cannot establish an edge. I found no systematic Nifty or Midcap/Smallcap 50/200 backtest with costs, taxes (STCG), and drawdowns.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Nifty 50 / Midcap 150 / Smallcap 250 Golden Cross backtest found. Local backtests (project code `backtest_golden_cross.py`) would fill this; not used here.
- Faber table numbers (CAGR, max drawdown for 1973-2012) were searched but not confirmed. Do not cite specific figures without opening the paper.

## 4. Risks of concentrated Indian small/mid-cap holdings: drawdowns

### Takeaway
Small caps fell far more than large caps in each stress episode and took years to recover (2008 more than 6 years, 2018 about 3 years). The 2024-26 episode: -24% in 2025 per DSP; index had recovered to within about 4% of its Sep 2024 high by 31 Jul 2026. Index-level drawdowns understate single-stock risk.

### Cited Findings
- Nifty Smallcap 250 TRI vs Nifty 50 TRI, calendar-year drawdowns (DSP, NSE data, as of 31 Mar 2026; fund sales page): 2008 -73% vs -59%; 2018 -36% vs -14%; 2020 -43% vs -38%; 2025 -24% vs -8%. — [DSP](https://www.dspim.com/smallcap-fund-page/truth-to-know) (fetched and read)
- Recovery to prior high, Smallcap 250 TRI (DSP, as of 31 Mar 2026): 2008 fall more than 6 years; 2018 fall about 3 years; 2024 fall still 18 months in (not yet recovered at that date). — same
- Max drawdown about -76% in 2008 on TRI (DSP deck) vs -73% calendar-year (above): the two measures differ. — [DSP NFO deck](https://www.dspim.com/media/pages/docs/dsp-nifty-smallcap-250-quality-50-index-nfo-presentation.pdf) (from search summary)
- Average recovery time of Smallcap 250 to previous highs: 853 days (vs 609 for momentum-quality variant). — [Mirae AM, June 2025](https://www.miraeassetmf.co.in/docs/default-source/product-presentations/mirae-asset-nifty-smallcap250-momentum-quality-100-etf-fof---june-2025.pdf) (fund marketing doc)
- Value Research: 2018 slide, then about 40% fall in Feb-Mar 2020; about 40 months to regain the starting level. — [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/226591/dark-side-small-cap-investing/) (search summary; page fetch 403)
- Conflicting: Basunivesh puts the 2020 small-cap fall at about 60% (vs 43% above); peak definition not given. — [Basunivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/who-can-invest-in-small-cap-funds/)
- Nifty Smallcap 250 2024-26: Sep 2024 high 18,623; level 17,920 on 31 Jul 2026, needing about 3.92% to regain. — [Cafemutual / Abakkus](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/38496-nifty-50-needs-8-gain-to-reclaim-its-peak-abakkus-mutual-fund) (agency-fed figures, UNVERIFIED vs NSE)
- Smallcap 250 fell 6.0% in CY2025, its weakest calendar year in seven years (Ambit, Aug 2026). That is a year return, not peak-to-trough. — [Ambit](https://www.ambit.co/newsletter/newsletter-august-2026/)
- Feb 2025: Smallcap 250 reported more than 20% down from peak (bear territory). — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/smallcap-stocks-slip-further-into-bear-grip-as-sell-off-continues-125021801080_1.html)
- Nifty Midcap 150, Apr 2005 to Aug 2026 (Abakkus): declines of 5-10% 17 times (about every 1.2 yrs); 10-20% 8 times (every 2.6 yrs); more than 20% 5 times (every 4.2 yrs). Index 23,537 on 31 Aug 2026, 0.01% short of completing recovery. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/mutual-funds/story/nifty-midcap-150-falls-over-20-once-every-4-2-years-abakkus-study-555987-2026-09-17)
- Nifty Midcap 150, 2018-2020 pieces: fell 27% from 2 Mar to 7 Apr 2020 (Capitalmind); annualised about -9% from Jan 2018 to Oct 2019 (NSE whitepaper); +186% from 23 Mar 2020 bottom to Oct 2021 (freefincal). — [Capitalmind](https://premium.capitalmind.in/2020/04/the-midcap-carnage-and-where-there-may-be-opportunity/); [freefincal](https://freefincal.com/why-this-market-crash-could-be-healthy-after-all/); [NSE whitepaper](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY_LargeMidcap_250_Whitepaper_s2.pdf)

### Inferences
- Fair view for holders: 2018 and 2008 show index drawdowns of 36% and 73% with multi-year recovery; this is the cost of buy-and-hold in small caps and is where timing rules claim value. It is also where a concentrated stock holder faces worse, as single stocks can fall further than the index and never recover (Bessembinder: 57% of US stocks lost to T-bills).
- Rebounds are sharp (+186% midcap in 18 months from the 2020 low), which supports both sides: missing it is costly, but surviving to 2020 required holding through a 40%+ fall.
- Calendar-year "drawdowns" (DSP) are not full cycle peak-to-trough; the 2018-2020 combined peak-to-trough was not found.

### Gaps
- No clean Smallcap 250 or Midcap 150 peak-to-trough for Jan 2018 to Mar 2020 and for Sep 2024 to trough (date and level of the 2025 low). Compute from NSE daily closes (niftyindices.com) before publishing.
- DSP, Mirae, Abakkus are fund houses; their charts select favourable framing.
- Single-stock small-cap drawdown/failure rates in India: no data found.

## Overall note for the report writer
Evidence supports time in the market over rapid trading, but (a) best-days charts are one-sided, (b) trend rules reduce drawdown more than they raise return, and (c) Indian small-cap holders faced 36-73% index drawdowns with 3 to 6+ year recoveries. For a concentrated portfolio, the Bessembinder skew result cuts both ways: do not sell winners early, but diversification or breadth is what improves odds of owning the rare big winner.
